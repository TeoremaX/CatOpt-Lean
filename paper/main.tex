\begin{abstract}
Metaheuristic optimizers are often presented as pipelines of reusable
operators---construction, perturbation, local improvement and acceptance.
Interpreting all operators as endomorphisms of a solution space supplies a
composition law but hides a crucial operational distinction: a perturbation
may worsen a candidate, whereas an elitist acceptance rule compares it with
the previous incumbent. We present a small, intentionally restricted formal
model in Lean~4 and Mathlib based on a category of feasibility-preserving
maps, an indexed syntax for phase-correct search pipelines, and a semantics
that preserves categorical composition equations. The proposed main theorem
states that the incumbent objective does not increase under any well-typed
pipeline when each primitive preserves feasibility and acceptance is elitist.
We express GRASP-like, fixed-schedule VNS and Iterated Greedy rounds using the
same syntax, and provide separate, non-verified TSP implementations in
Python for exploratory empirical analysis. We distinguish syntax-level
compositionality from the construction of an actual free category and avoid
claims of stochastic convergence or algorithmic novelty. Crucially, at the
time of this draft the Lean source has \emph{not yet been compiled in the
authoring environment}; it is a candidate formalization, not a reported
kernel-checked proof result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Optimization frameworks routinely combine building blocks. A variable
neighborhood search (VNS) method may perturb a solution, descend to a local
minimum and apply an acceptance criterion; an Iterated Greedy method may
destroy a partial solution and repair it before descent. GRASP alternates
greedy-randomized construction and local improvement. The attraction of a
common language is clear: operators could be selected, composed, checked and
possibly synthesized.

A first proposal would declare $\operatorname{Shake},\operatorname{LS},
\operatorname{Accept}:S\rightarrow S$ and write
$\operatorname{VNS}=\operatorname{Accept}\circ
\operatorname{LS}\circ\operatorname{Shake}$. This yields an endomorphism
monoid, but essentially every program is a function that composes with
other functions. The category-theoretic vocabulary has not yet supplied a
constraint, theorem or design principle. Moreover, a unary acceptance
function operating only on the perturbed solution cannot, in general,
compare that candidate to an earlier incumbent unless extra state is
provided.

Our contribution is therefore limited but specific: we separate incumbent
and candidate; require certified feasibility-preserving primitive functions;
restrict well-formed pipelines with phase-indexed types; provide a denotational
categorical interpretation; and state an incumbent monotonicity theorem
parametric in the primitive implementations. These ideas should be regarded
as a foundation for investigation rather than a novel categorical result.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The Lean library targets natural-number-valued single-objective minimization
with elitist acceptance, not stochastic convergence or all possible
metaheuristics. The experimental Python algorithms are not extracted from
Lean and are not covered by the formal statements. We do not claim
outperformance, novelty or verified correctness of external TSP code.

\section{Three candidate mathematical models}
We evaluated three starting points.

\paragraph{Unrestricted endomorphisms.}
For a solution type $S$, let arrows be functions $S\to S$; their composition
is associative and has the identity map. This is useful for an ordinary
functional implementation, but offers no protection against infeasible
solutions, no typed distinction between search phases, and no nontrivial
relation between candidate and incumbent. Properties such as ``both maps
are non-worsening, therefore so is their composition'' follow immediately
from order transitivity, not from an essential new categorical insight.

\paragraph{Invariant-preserving maps and indexed syntax.}
Instead, equip each state type with a feasibility predicate and restrict
arrows to maps preserving it. Make algorithm steps an indexed syntax with
explicit input/output phase types. Distinguish an incumbent from a candidate
and make acceptance the only primitive allowed to update the incumbent.
This formulation provides useful, compositional correctness contracts and
can be encoded directly in Lean. It is selected for the prototype.

\paragraph{Probabilistic effects.}
A genuinely stochastic operator is more naturally a kernel
$S\to\mathcal{D}(S)$ or a Kleisli arrow for a suitable distribution
monad. The composition law must account for dependent sampling. Expected
progress, irreducibility, ergodicity or stochastic approximation would then
require additional assumptions. The current Lean prototype instead uses
explicit seed arguments to deterministic arrows and makes no claim of a
probability-measure semantics. Implementing this third approach too early
would add complexity without substantiating the initial invariant theorem.

\section{Category of feasible spaces}
\begin{definition}[Feasible space]
A feasible space is a pair $X=(|X|,F_X)$ consisting of a type $|X|$ and a
predicate $F_X:|X|\rightarrow\mathrm{Prop}$.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Feasibility-preserving morphism]
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ consists of a total map
$|f|:|X|\to |Y|$ with a witness of
\begin{equation}
 \forall x\in |X|,\quad F_X(x)\Rightarrow F_Y(f(x)).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Identity is the identity function with its trivial preservation proof.
For $f:X\to Y$ and $g:Y\to Z$ define $(g\circ f)(x)=g(f(x))$;
feasibility of its result follows by applying the witnesses for $f$ and
$g$ in sequence.

\begin{proposition}[Category laws]
Feasible spaces and feasibility-preserving morphisms form a category
$\mathsf{Feas}$ under the proposed identity and composition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each feasible $x$, identity preserves $F_X(x)$. Composition preserves
feasibility as above. The underlying maps satisfy
$\mathrm{id}\circ f=f=f\circ\mathrm{id}$ and
$(h\circ g)\circ f=h\circ(g\circ f)$ by function extensionality.
Witness fields are propositions and are proof-irrelevant, so equality of
underlying maps suffices for equality of morphism structures. The Lean
prototype defines a `CategoryTheory.Category' instance using these laws.
\end{proof}

This category is a familiar construction, not an invention. Its role is to
record and compose data-independent feasibility guarantees for typed
interfaces. It supports maps between different state spaces, although the
first search DSL interprets each phase with the same proof-carrying runtime
carrier.

\section{Problems, operators and state}
A minimization problem is a triple $P=(S,F,f)$, where $S$ is a state type,
$F:S\to\mathrm{Prop}$ is feasibility, and $f:S\to\mathbb{N}$ is the
objective. A legal solution operator is a feasible endomorphism of
$(S,F)$; such an operator need not reduce $f$. Let
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{NW}(u)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall x, F(x)\Rightarrow f(u(x))\le f(x).
\end{equation}
For legal $u$ and $v$, if both are non-worsening then
$f(v(u(x)))\le f(u(x))\le f(x)$, so $v\circ u$ is non-worsening.
An elementary induction gives this property for any finite iteration.

More important is the two-track search state
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{SearchState}(P)=\{(i,c):S\times S\mid F(i)\land F(c)\},
\end{equation}
with incumbent $i$ and candidate $c$.
For a certified feasible primitive $u:S\to S$ define
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{reset}_u(i,c)&=(i,u(i)),\\
 \mathrm{transform}_u(i,c)&=(i,u(c)).
\end{align}
The first is appropriate for constructing or perturbing a fresh candidate
from the incumbent, while the second models candidate-only descent.

Define a deterministic elitist choice
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{choose}(i,c)=
 \begin{cases}
 c &\text{if }f(c)\le f(i),\\
 i &\text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Acceptance is
$\mathrm{accept}(i,c)=(\mathrm{choose}(i,c),\mathrm{choose}(i,c))$.
Both selected states are feasible and
\begin{equation}
 f(\mathrm{choose}(i,c))\le f(i).
\end{equation}
Crucially, there is no restriction on whether $f(u(i))$ may be larger
than $f(i)$ for a perturbation $u$.

\begin{theorem}[Monotone incumbent for a single search round]
For any legal perturbation $p$ and candidate-local operator $d$, the
composite $\mathrm{accept}\circ\mathrm{transform}_{d}\circ
\mathrm{reset}_{p}$ does not increase incumbent objective.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both `reset' and `transform' leave the incumbent field unchanged.
The final acceptance selects a state no more expensive than the previous
incumbent. Therefore the composite is incumbent-monotone, independently of
the quality of $p$ or $d$.
\end{proof}

\section{A phase-typed language}
Let the set of phases be
$\mathsf{Stage}=\{\mathsf{ready},\mathsf{candidate},\mathsf{improved}\}$.
The typed generator signature contains:
\begin{align*}
 \mathrm{construct}(s)&:\mathsf{ready}\to\mathsf{candidate},\\
 \mathrm{shake}(k)&:\mathsf{ready}\to\mathsf{candidate},\\
 \mathrm{destroyRepair}(k)&:\mathsf{ready}\to\mathsf{candidate},\\
 \mathrm{localSearch}&:\mathsf{candidate}\to\mathsf{improved},\\
 \mathrm{accept}&:\mathsf{improved}\to\mathsf{ready}.
\end{align*}
The indices enforce that the output phase of one operation equals the input
phase of the next. In particular, a program attempting to accept before
local search fails to typecheck. This restriction is intentional; not every
operational search scheme passes through this precise three-step protocol.

\begin{definition}[Typed program]
Given a signature $\Sigma$ assigning primitive edges $\Sigma(a,b)$, the
type $\mathrm{Program}_{\Sigma}(a,b)$ is freely generated as a syntax tree
by $\mathrm{skip}:a\to a$, atomic edges
$\mathrm{atom}(e):a\to b$, and composition
$\mathrm{seq}(p,q):a\to c$ when $p:a\to b$ and $q:b\to c$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Not yet a free category.}
The raw AST constructors do not equate
$\mathrm{seq}(\mathrm{seq}(p,q),r)$ with
$\mathrm{seq}(p,\mathrm{seq}(q,r))$.
Thus calling the raw syntax a strict category would be incorrect.
A categorical interpretation obeys equations even when the syntax does not.
A future quotient by associativity and units, or a representation as paths,
would give the corresponding free category and enable an explicit Mathlib
`Functor' record.

\section{Semantics and generic soundness}
Given a semantic category $\mathcal{C}$, map each syntactic object $a$ to
an object $I(a)$, and each generator $e:a\to b$ to an arrow
$I(e):I(a)\to I(b)$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \den{\mathrm{skip}}=\mathrm{id},\qquad
 \den{\mathrm{atom}(e)}=I(e),\qquad
 \den{\mathrm{seq}(p,q)}=\den q\circ\den p.
\end{equation}
The associativity and unit equations hold by the category axioms.
For search programs, each runtime search state is a proof-carrying object;
thus any total function between two such state types is feasibility-preserving
with respect to the exterior predicate `True'. Real candidate/incumbent
feasibility is stored in the internal dependent record rather than inferred
from `True' itself. This choice separates protocol indexing from runtime
invariants and exposes an area for future heterogeneous-type work.

\begin{theorem}[Parametric incumbent monotonicity]
Let $P$ be a minimization problem and let the concrete generator assignments
map each construction, shake, destroy--repair and local-search primitive to
a feasibility-preserving operator. Assume `accept' is the elitist rule above.
Then for every well-typed search program $p:a\to b$ and search state $s$,
\begin{equation}
 f\bigl(\mathrm{incumbent}(\den{p}(s))\bigr)
 \le f\bigl(\mathrm{incumbent}(s)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By induction on syntax. The identity program leaves its input unchanged.
Every atomic construction/perturbation/candidate descent leaves the
incumbent unchanged; the acceptance atom never worsens it. If $p$ and
$q$ separately satisfy the property, then
\[
 f(I(q(p(s))))\le f(I(p(s)))\le f(I(s)).
\]
Transitivity yields the composite case. No assumption is needed that
perturbations or descent procedures improve the candidate.
\end{proof}

The proof is useful but not a convergence theorem. It does not imply that
any step improves the incumbent strictly. A sequence can stagnate forever;
finite or infinite execution may remain in suboptimal regions. If the
acceptance policy sometimes admits a worse incumbent, as in simulated
annealing, the theorem must be weakened or changed.

\section{Algorithm representations}
The generic syntax represents a GRASP-style round by
\[\mathrm{construct}(s)\,;\,\mathrm{localSearch}\,;\,\mathrm{accept}.\]
A fixed-schedule VNS round is
\[\mathrm{shake}(k)\,;\,\mathrm{localSearch}\,;\,\mathrm{accept}.\]
The Iterated Greedy analogue is
\[\mathrm{destroyRepair}(d)\,;\,\mathrm{localSearch}\,;\,\mathrm{accept}.\]
Program sequencing and finite folds combine rounds without changing the
generic soundness theorem. However, real adaptive GVNS changes the radius
based on the accepted result; this control structure is absent from the
current Lean DSL. A Lean finite-state demonstration has a single bounded
descent operator and is not full VND. The separate Python implementations
use a genuine two-neighborhood (2-opt and insertion) VND and an adaptive
GVNS radius; no refinement or semantic-equivalence proof links them to
Lean. Those limitations are important whenever using the repo in a
research presentation.

\section{Experimental protocol and preliminary measurements}
As a proof-of-execution check we implemented symmetric, closed-tour TSP
solvers in Python, using deterministic seeds and three original small
coordinate datasets. The objective is the sum of rounded Euclidean edge
lengths. Held--Karp dynamic programming computes exact optimum costs on
each dataset. A full objective-evaluation counter limits runs; the same
budget is used for each method, but greedy construction's distance accesses
are not charged as complete objective evaluations.

The first smoke experiment used three seeds, 1500 full objective evaluations
and two small instances. On \texttt{grid12} the exact optimum was 214 and
GRASP--VND achieved 214 for all three seeds; GVNS achieved the optimum in
two of three and Iterated Greedy in one of three. On \texttt{ring14} the
exact optimum was 236; the respective optimal-hit counts were three, two
and two. These numbers are sanity checks, not a statistically defensible
comparison of algorithm quality. The full table, including means and
standard deviations, is in \texttt{docs/experiments.md}.

Meaningful analysis needs many more seeds, diverse instances, fair accounting
for computational effort, baseline algorithms, ablation studies and
confidence intervals. We should expect conclusions to depend on tuning,
instance class and budget. No claim is made here that a categorical model
improves empirical quality.

\section{Related work and positioning}
Fong, Spivak and Tuy\'eras~\cite{fong2019backprop} established a genuine
compositional connection between parameterized functions and learning
updates. Gavranovi\'c~\cite{gavranovic2024fundamental} develops broader
compositional machinery for deep learning, while
Gavranovi\'c et al.~\cite{gavranovic2024architectures} study algebraic
architecture constraints. Shiebler~\cite{shiebler2021generalized}
explores generalizations of derivative-based optimization and their
invariances. These sources illuminate what a mathematically substantial
categorical result can look like, but none is simply a certificate of novelty
for the present optimization DSL.

Before a research contribution can be claimed, a systematic comparison with
hyper-heuristics, grammar-guided algorithm design, typed genetic programming,
automated configuration and formal verification of optimization algorithms
is mandatory. The current results are closely aligned with familiar
techniques from dependent types, program logic and algebraic semantics.
It would be mistaken to argue that the presence of a category or functor-like
interpretation alone makes the framework novel or algorithmically stronger.

\section{Directions for stronger contributions}
The first extension is to quotient or normalize compositional syntax and
build an explicit functor from its path category to the category of feasible
state transformations. Distinct state types (populations, partial solutions,
individual solutions and archive summaries) would demonstrate where a typed
categorical interface gives more than a set of endomorphisms.

Second, contracts could describe resources and uncertainty. A graded effect
could accumulate objective evaluations or probabilistic sampling cost.
For non-elitist acceptance, a useful theorem might bound expected potential
instead of demanding monotonicity on every run. Formalizing these effects
would clarify whether categories enriched in preorders or Kleisli categories
provide real benefits.

Third, a well-typed synthesis engine could enumerate or optimize candidate
algorithms while rejecting infeasible or ill-formed compositions. Sound
rewrites would avoid searching different syntax trees with the same
semantics. Demonstrating practical benefit would require comparing this
process with established grammar-guided hyper-heuristics, not simply
reporting the generation of new strings of operators.

Finally, a Lean-certified TSP permutation type, verified 2-opt/insertion
operators and a formal connection to executable code would upgrade this
from a theorem about an abstract DSL into a more faithful optimizer
verification. Success would require proving invariants at the interfaces
where correctness currently rests only on Python tests.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
The framework proves only properties induced by its specific definitions
and primitive contracts. In particular, no probabilistic semantics,
global optimality, runtime bound, strict improvement or guarantee of
algorithm discovery follows. The three algorithm families share a
restricted protocol rather than a complete behavioral semantics.

The principal technical point is the separation of candidate and incumbent:
it makes it mathematically honest to permit worsening perturbations while
certifying non-worsening elitist incumbent trajectories. Typed composition
makes this property modular. The categorical structure gives a uniform
semantic language but has not yet yielded a theorem inaccessible to simpler
algebraic reasoning.

At the date of this manuscript, \textbf{Lean compilation has not been run in
the authoring environment}. The project therefore contains proposed
formal proof scripts awaiting kernel checking in CI, not a completed
verification claim. The Python benchmark unit tests have run successfully;
their outcomes are deliberately reported separately from formal results.

\section{Proof obligations and the software architecture}

A formalization of the preceding mathematical objects must distinguish
three forms of correctness. First, \emph{well-formedness} says that an
operation's input and output phase indices match. Second, \emph{feasibility}
requires that a primitive map transport proofs of the relevant admissibility
predicate. Third, \emph{performance contracts} concern the cost of an
incumbent rather than candidate feasibility. The implementation stores these
obligations in different locations so a future extension can replace one
without silently weakening another.

The central Lean modules are:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{3.2cm}p{9.0cm}}
\toprule
Module & Responsibility \\
\midrule
\texttt{Basic} & Explicit objective and dependent candidate/incumbent feasibility proofs \\
\texttt{Category} & Invariant-preserving arrows, identity, composition and Mathlib category instance \\
\texttt{Operators} & Non-worsening predicate, closure under composition, finite iteration \\
\texttt{Properties} & Reset, candidate-only transformation, acceptance, incumbent monotonicity \\
\texttt{DSL.Syntax} & Typed generic abstract syntax trees \\
\texttt{DSL.Semantics} & Interpretation into semantic categories and equational laws \\
\texttt{DSL.Search} & Phase-specific primitive signature, categorical interpretation, structural theorem \\
\texttt{Algorithms} & Three compositional program-building schemes \\
\texttt{Examples} & Concrete 21-state demonstration, not a benchmark optimizer \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Interface-level proof reuse.}
Suppose a new search space is introduced with a strong geometric constraint,
e.g. permutations that visit every city exactly once. Once a primitive
operator has supplied a map and a feasibility-preservation witness, the
search-level theorem does not inspect how that primitive was implemented.
A construction might use greedy restricted candidate lists; perturbation
might use random swaps; repair might use specialized insertion. These
implementations contribute obligations to prove tour feasibility, not a
new copy of the composite monotonicity proof.

\paragraph{What Lean must actually check.}
In particular, the authoring process cannot establish the absence of
elaboration or universe issues by visually inspecting a code listing.
The intended acceptance test is that \texttt{lake build} successfully
elaborates and kernel-checks every declaration, including category instances,
indexed interpretations and all regression examples, against a pinned Lean
and Mathlib dependency graph. The auxiliary source scan detects unfinished
proof tokens but proves no theorem. The two checks should not be conflated.

\section{A minimal counterexample and the correct invariant}
Consider the feasible domain $S=\{0,\ldots,20\}$ with objective $f(x)=x$.
Let the perturbation be $p(x)=(x+1)\bmod 21$. Then
$f(p(0))=1>0=f(0)$, so $p$ is not non-worsening. Nonetheless, $p$ maps
feasible inputs to feasible outputs. The model intentionally permits it
as a categorical morphism; otherwise a framework advertised for
perturbation-based metaheuristics would exclude its principal building
block.

Initialize the two-track state at $(i,c)=(0,0)$, reset the candidate using
$p$, apply any feasible candidate descent $d$ and finally accept. If
the transformed candidate $d(1)$ has cost at most zero, it becomes the
new incumbent; otherwise the incumbent remains zero. In both cases
$\operatorname{cost}(i')\le\operatorname{cost}(i)$. The preservation result
rests on the selection rule rather than the perturbation's properties.

This separation also clarifies a false extension: if an acceptance rule
allows worsening incumbents with positive probability, its trajectory is
not pathwise non-worsening. An assertion about expected cost would be a
separate theorem, typically requiring a probability distribution on
transitions, integrability and a suitable drift assumption. A deterministic
seed value does not by itself supply this mathematical structure.

\section{Worked example of syntax and interpretation}

A composition of a fixed GRASP-like round and a VNS-like round has the
shape
\begin{align*}
 p={}&\mathrm{seq}\bigl(\mathrm{seq}(\mathrm{construct}(s),\mathrm{localSearch}),
        \mathrm{accept}\bigr);\\
 q={}&\mathrm{seq}\bigl(\mathrm{seq}(\mathrm{shake}(k),\mathrm{localSearch}),
        \mathrm{accept}\bigr).
\end{align*}
Both start and end in the \emph{ready} phase, so $p;q$ and $q;p$ are
well-typed. The type system alone does not assert these two programs are
semantically equivalent; order can affect the resulting incumbent and
runtime. It only supplies valid intermediate interfaces and compositional
meaning.

Because the interpreter is defined structurally, reassociation of syntax
leaves its denotation unchanged:
\[
 \den{(p;q);r}=\den{r}\circ(\den{q}\circ\den{p})
                   =(\den{r}\circ\den{q})\circ\den{p}
                   =\den{p;(q;r)}.
\]
This is an example of a \emph{semantic equation} proved from category laws.
The unquotiented expressions remain distinct AST values. Extending the
library with a quotient or a normal form for typed paths would allow such
equations to justify the automatic elimination of redundant syntactic
programs in future synthesis experiments.

There is an important subtlety about ``stage as object.'' Each stage
currently maps to the same proof-carrying runtime carrier; different stage
names are a protocol distinction in syntax, not necessarily different
semantic types. This semantics is non-faithful in general and cannot by
itself demonstrate the benefits of heterogeneous object composition.
A meaningful next test would compose a builder of partial TSP tours with
a repairer producing feasible complete tours, and then a local search
operator whose domain is restricted to complete permutations.

\section{Experimental reproducibility and threats to validity}

In the numerical study, $n$ is small enough for an exact Held--Karp
benchmark. Full tours use a fixed origin city, visit all other cities once,
and return to the origin. Distances use rounded Euclidean edge weights.
Holding the full-objective-evaluation limit constant makes comparisons
more intelligible, but it is not equivalent to holding runtime constant:
greedy construction, perturbation, and insertion neighborhoods perform
many edge-distance queries, and different implementations incur different
Python overhead. Therefore, the experiment logs an evaluation budget rather
than claiming equal computational complexity or fair wall-clock cost.

The larger executed reference batch used 15 seeds and 5000 objective
calls. Its outcomes were:

\small
\begin{tabular}{llrrr}
\toprule
Instance (optimal cost) & Method & Mean cost & Standard deviation & Optimal hits \\
\midrule
\texttt{grid12} (214) & GRASP--VND & 214.00 & 0.00 & 15/15 \\
 & GVNS & 214.47 & 1.75 & 14/15 \\
 & Iterated Greedy & 216.93 & 4.23 & 10/15 \\
\texttt{ring14} (236) & GRASP--VND & 236.00 & 0.00 & 15/15 \\
 & GVNS & 236.00 & 0.00 & 15/15 \\
 & Iterated Greedy & 237.00 & 2.00 & 12/15 \\
\texttt{cluster16} (304) & GRASP--VND & 304.00 & 0.00 & 15/15 \\

The table chiefly reveals a \emph{ceiling effect}: almost all methods
solve these small synthetic examples optimally. Thus it is a useful
reproducibility exercise and a poor discriminator of method quality.
A larger evaluation should record heterogeneous instances, algorithm
parameters, seed pairing, wall-clock and memory measures, confidence
intervals, and references for best-known values. Publication-grade claims
would require a new benchmark design.
